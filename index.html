Rehearse before execute · from candidates to outcomes

Before choosing a recovery action, inspect what it will do. A degraded UAV may still continue its mission, return, or land, but fault severity alone does not reveal the consequences of these choices. Our digital twin rehearses candidate commands through flight-control-compatible interfaces; a shared evaluator compares their responses before one action is selected for execution. This overview follows the complete story—from motivation and method to flight-response comparisons, recovery selection, closed-loop experiments, and physical demonstrations. The aim is to retain recoverable missions while intervening when needed. In repeated mild-fault trials far from home, Proposed achieves 96.7% mission progress versus 36.1%–37.7% for the threshold strategy, with controlled recovery in all ten trials for both. Other tested cases show scenario-specific benefits of selective intervention.

Selection without rehearsal vs. DT rehearsal

Left — Selection without explicit rehearsal: predefined contingency logic or a learned policy maps the observed condition to an action. The decision can be efficient, but does not necessarily expose comparable trajectories for the alternatives.

Right — Rehearse before selection: the DT virtually executes each configured candidate under the current degraded condition. The shared evaluator compares the responses and selects one command for physical execution. The purpose is to inspect action-dependent consequences, rather than infer them solely from an action label.

What was tested, and what each result means

The six scenarios cover early/late mission stages, steady/maneuvering flight, and near-/far-home motion. The 102 executions comprise 36 exploratory trials, 60 additional far-home trials, and six fixed-strategy late-mission trials. Reused records are counted once. The late-mission comparison has one execution per strategy and fault level; the mild far-home comparison has ten per strategy.

Recovery manoeuvres have their own dynamic costs. In the mild case, Return reaches a larger acceleration peak than Continue despite being a more conservative mission mode. In the severe case, Land has the lowest candidate cost and a shorter recorded flight duration. Together with response fidelity and component analysis, physical execution motivates considering both continued-flight degradation and the consequences of intervention. The study covers graded accelerometer noise, closed-loop simulation, and indoor physical executions. Broader faults, outdoor missions, and physical end-to-end activation remain future work.
